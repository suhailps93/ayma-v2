Automatic analysis of your photos and public information

To build your profile and to improve the quality of the matches we suggest, Ayma automatically analyses:

  • Photos you upload — including facial analysis and physique (body) analysis of the photos you add to your profile, send in chat, or send in direct messages.
  • Information about you that is publicly available online — Ayma performs a web search for your name and reads what it finds on the open web (for example professional pages, social profiles, news, personal sites).

This analysis is always and only about you, the account holder — your own photos and your own name. Ayma does not run this automatic analysis on other people, on your matches, or on anyone you are talking to.

The results are used by Ayma to understand you better and to match you more accurately. They are kept in Ayma's own working memory and are not shown in your visible profile unless you ask for them — for example by opening a facial or physique report, or by asking Ayma to look you up on the web. When you ask, that result is added to your profile story; until then it stays private to Ayma.

You cannot turn this analysis off while you use Ayma. It is a core part of how the service works. If you do not want your photos and public information analysed in this way, please do not use Ayma. You can delete your account at any time, which removes this data as described below.

Biometric information (face matching)

Ayma uses face recognition. Before you sign in you give a separate, explicit consent on the sign-in page (its own checkbox, apart from accepting these Terms and the Privacy Policy). Ayma cannot be used without it.

  • What we collect: from the photos you add (profile photos, your verification selfie, and photos you share with Ayma) we create face data, a numerical description of your face (a "face embedding"). It is not a photo and it is never shown to anyone.
  • Why, including matching against the public web: to check that a photo really shows you. We match your face data against photos found on the public web for your web search and deep dives, against the results of a reverse-image search of your own photos, against photos you share with Ayma, and against other accounts' verification selfies so one person cannot claim the free starter credits more than once. When another Ayma member runs a deep dive on you from Explore, your face data is used the same way, so the photos shown to them are actually you. Deep dives only ever look at Ayma members who have given this consent.
  • What we keep: your face data, plus the links to web photos that matched you and how closely they matched. We do not keep copies of web photos.
  • Retention: we keep your face data only while your account exists. When your account is deleted, your face data is deleted with it, on the schedule in "How long we keep your data" in the Privacy Policy.
  • Withdrawal and deletion: you can withdraw this consent at any time in Settings, under "Face matching consent". Because face matching is part of how Ayma works, withdrawing deletes your face data straight away and then deletes your account. You can also simply sign out.
  • No sale, no advertising: we never sell, lease or trade your face data, never use it for advertising, and only disclose it to the service providers that process it for us (Google, see the subprocessor list) or where the law requires.

Connecting Google (Gmail, Calendar)

You can choose to connect a Google account so Ayma can learn real facts about you instead of only learning from conversation. This is optional and off by default — nothing is read from Google unless you connect an account, and you see what will be accessed before you approve it in Google's own consent screen.

If you connect, Ayma requests read-only access to:

  • Gmail — recent message subjects and senders for a bounded recent window of messages, plus the plain-text body of a small number of those messages. Ayma skips senders on an exclude list that starts with major banks, payment processors, and government/tax domains, and which we can extend at any time.
  • Google Calendar — recent and upcoming event titles and how many people are on each. Ayma does not read attendee identities or event descriptions.

(Ayma previously also requested a count of your recent Google Photos activity, never the photos themselves. That has been removed — Google no longer lets a third-party app like Ayma read a user's existing photo library. You can still add photos to Ayma directly, as described above.)

Ayma only ever looks at a bounded, recent window of this data — never your full history — and does not store your emails or calendar events. An AI pass reads what was pulled, once, and distills a short set of facts about you (for example "travels often for work" or "close with family") into your private AI notes, the same working memory described above. Those notes default to private to you — not shown on your public profile — and are never shown to your matches unless you choose to move them there. The underlying content pulled from Google is discarded after that pass; only the distilled facts remain. Data from your Google account, and the facts distilled from it, are never used for advertising.

You can disconnect a connected Google account at any time from Profile settings. Disconnecting revokes Ayma's access with Google immediately and stops any further reads; facts Ayma already learned stay in your notes, the same as anything else you've told Ayma, unless you edit or remove them yourself.
